Can a pattern make a coin easier to predict?

This is a software experiment, not a simulation of tumbling coin physics. Select independent fair outcomes or a deliberately small repeating tendency. The neural network’s weights stay fixed; your choices do not train it.

Changing either setting resets the session so results from different generators are not mixed. For the persistent setting, “always predict the previous face” has 51.5% expected accuracy. That is a stronger comparison than a 50% fair guess.

The model score is an uncalibrated neural-network output, not a promise that its guess is correct. The gauge called balance entropy measures the mix of outcomes, not their unpredictability.

What should you learn from this?

Balance is not randomness

The balance-entropy gauge uses only the proportions of heads and tails. 01010101 scores 1 bit because half the entries are heads, even though the next entry is predictable. This gauge ignores order.

A fair coin has no exploitable history

For independent fair outcomes, any predictor based only on the past has 50% expected accuracy. Finite samples can finish above or below 50%; that alone proves no advantage.

A constructed pattern needs its own baseline

With 51.5% persistence, always repeating the previous face already has 51.5% expected accuracy. Compare the model with that strategy, not just with random guessing.

Noise needs a definition

Flipping a percentage of binary labels is not the same measurement as audio signal-to-noise power. For power SNR, 10 log10(signal/noise), 0 dB means equal powers. The old unsupported noise-sweep table has been removed.
