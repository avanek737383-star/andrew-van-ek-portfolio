3D Planetary Radar & Global Internet Backbone

An interactive WebGL 3D planetary radar mapping the physical underpinnings of the global internet: imported subsea cable paths, coastal landing points, and interconnection reference locations with ground-level street exploration.

A schematic of Internet connectivity—not a live traffic radar.

Rotate the globe and select a cable or reference location to explore the geography. Animated dots, elevated arcs, colors, and sizes are illustrative. They are not measured packets, current throughput, exact physical circuits, or a claim that a particular share of world traffic passes through one node.

An Internet exchange is a place where networks interconnect; it is not automatically a Tier-1 transit network. The imported cable geometry is a reference snapshot, not a live cable-status feed.

      What do the lines and locations mean?

      Internet links run through physical cables and network sites. This globe combines saved cable geometry and landing-point coordinates with a small set of interconnection reference locations.

      Subsea cable paths

      The saved TeleGeography geometry shows cable routes as a geographic reference. Moving dashes help you follow a line; they do not show real photons, packets, traffic volume or exact seabed cable positions.

      Coastal landing points and street imagery

      Select a landing point to inspect the imported coordinates and open links to nearby street imagery. Available imagery may not show the actual cable landing facility.

      Interconnection geography

      The marked reference locations include Ashburn, Frankfurt, Amsterdam and London. Elevated arcs illustrate connections between places; they are not measured circuits or live capacity readings.

      How the country map and sunlight shading work

      Country outlines and names use a bundled Natural Earth 1:110m snapshot. Zoom in for smaller labels or hover a country to read its name. The current UTC time feeds an approximate solar-position calculation, with a soft blend between the light and dark map styles. The map-style button also lets you choose an entirely light or dark map.

      Sources: Natural Earth map data and NOAA solar-position equations. Country shading is a presentation of daylight, separate from the site’s seasonal theme.
